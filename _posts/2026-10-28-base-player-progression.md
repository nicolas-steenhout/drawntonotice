A friend asked me if I could to a progression series of photos for a sketch. She asked for a time lapse, but that's a bit too much setup right now. So here is progression for this little sketch!

![Ink and wash sketch of an upright bass player. He's a man with curly hair, he's looking down while playing a warm reddish-brown upright bass, set against a dark background.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-01.jpg)

I did this one from a photo. The sketch doesn't look like the photo, and I'm not sure I care all that much. It is practice runs in a "scratch" sketchbook after all.

I did this over two days, a few sessions at a time. Basically getting sidetracked on other "quests" (like doing dishes or having a bath) while waiting for things to dry. I actually spent maybe 20 minutes on this of active work.

![Reference photo of a man playing the upright bass in a dark room.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-02.jpg)

I'm not entirely sure why I wanted to do someone playing the upright bass.

## First, pencil

I started by drawing the general shape I wanted with pencil. I know, some people recommend going straight to ink. And I do that as well, but not here!

![Initial sketch done in pencil. General shapes, no details.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-03.jpg)

## Second, ink

I used a Pentel pocket ink brush for this one. I tend to use a TWSBI Eco fountain pen with De Atramentis sepia ink. But I want to get some more practice with the brush pen. Which uses black ink. So a different experience.

The line variation that's possible with a brush pen is significantly bigger than with an EF fountain pen.

![The same subject, but this time I added black ink lines. Lines have varying thicknesses.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-04.jpg)

## Third, colors

This is where I add layers of colors. Many layers. Even though the paper I use is a bit shite (Moleskin sketch paper). It's good for a few layers, but not great.

I started by adding some color on the upright bass. 

![The same subject again. This time I added some burnt sienna on the bass.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-05.jpg)

Next, added skin tones and hair. I typically use raw sienna and rubby red for skin. But I already had some burnt sienna I was using, so that's the red I used for skin.

![Skin tones were added, and the player's hair.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-06.jpg)

Next I darkened the shadows. And added shadows and wrinkles to the white shirt. Added the jeans as well.

![Same subject, with shadows added to the player's shirt, and color for his jeans. Additional layers of colors on the bass.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-07.jpg)

And more layers of colors. Deepening the shadows. Creating contrast. Adding depth to the sketch.

![And again, just adding layers of colors to create contrast and depth.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-08.jpg)

And finally, adding the dark background. No blacks were used in the making of this sketch! Mix of ultramarine blue, raw umber, and burnt sienna got me there. This was a progression of three wet-on-wet washes, getting progressively darker.

![Ink and wash sketch of an upright bass player. He's a man with curly hair, he's looking down while playing a warm reddish-brown upright bass, set against a dark background.](/images/2026-10-28-progression-01.jpg)
